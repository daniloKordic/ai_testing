# Carrots

## Overview
Carrots are root vegetables belonging to the Apiaceae family, known for their vibrant orange color and sweet, earthy flavor. They are one of the most widely cultivated and consumed vegetables worldwide, available in various colors including purple, red, and yellow.

## Varieties
- **Nantes**: Cylindrical, sweet, and commonly used for fresh eating
- **Imperator**: Long, tapered carrots popular in the US market
- **Chantenay**: Shorter, stout carrots with a blunt end
- **Paris Market (Thumbelina)**: Small, round baby carrots
- **Purple**: Heirloom variety with deep purple exterior and orange interior
- **Yellow/White**: Mild, sweet varieties less common in modern cultivation

## Nutritional Profile
- Exceptionally high in beta-carotene (vitamin A precursor)
- Good source of vitamin K, potassium, and fiber
- Contain biotin and vitamin C
- Low in calories (about 41 calories per 100g)
- Rich in antioxidants including lycopene and lutein
- Contains polyphenols with anti-inflammatory properties

## Health Benefits
- Supports eye health and vision, especially night vision
- Promotes healthy skin with vitamin A and antioxidants
- Supports immune system function
- May reduce risk of heart disease
- Promotes digestive health with dietary fiber
- May help regulate blood sugar levels
- Contains compounds that may reduce cancer risk
- Supports bone health with vitamin K

## Growing Information
- **Climate**: Prefer cool season crops; tolerate temperatures 60-70°F (15-21°C)
- **Soil**: Well-drained, loose soil free of rocks and debris
- **Depth**: Root crops need 8-12 inches of soil depth
- **Water**: Moderate, consistent moisture; avoid waterlogging
- **Sunlight**: Full sun (6+ hours daily)
- **Harvest Time**: 60-80 days from planting; can be harvested early for baby carrots

## Storage
- Remove tops before storing to prevent moisture loss
- Store in refrigerator in a plastic bag for up to 3 weeks
- Can be frozen after blanching for up to 1 year
- Store separately from ethylene-producing fruits
- Keep in cool, humid environment for best results

## Culinary Uses
- Raw in salads, slaws, and as crunchy snacks
- Roasted with herbs and olive oil
- Steamed or boiled for side dishes
- Juiced for fresh carrot juice
- Used in soups, stews, and broths
- Incorporated into baked goods like carrot cake
- Grated into rice dishes and pasta
- Pickled for preserves
- Used in Asian stir-fries
