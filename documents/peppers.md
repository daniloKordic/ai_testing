# Peppers

## Overview
Peppers are hollow fruits (botanically) from the nightshade family, available in sweet and spicy varieties. They come in multiple colors representing different ripeness stages and flavor profiles, making them versatile ingredients in cuisines worldwide.

## Sweet Pepper Varieties
- **Bell Pepper**: Large, mild, sweet peppers in red, yellow, orange, green, purple
- **Poblano**: Large, dark green pepper with moderate heat; often used for roasting
- **Banana**: Long, thin, yellow pepper with mild sweetness
- **Anaheim**: Medium heat, used fresh or for roasting
- **Pimento**: Small, sweet, deeply red pepper, often roasted

## Hot/Spicy Pepper Varieties
- **Jalapeño**: Medium heat (2,500-8,000 SHU); popular in Mexican cuisine
- **Serrano**: Hot (10,000-23,000 SHU); smaller than jalapeños
- **Habanero**: Very hot (100,000-350,000 SHU); fruity flavor
- **Thai**: Small, extremely hot; commonly used in Asian cuisine
- **Cayenne**: Medium-hot, often dried and ground into powder

## Color Progression
- **Green**: Unripe, more bitter, higher in chlorophyll
- **Red**: Fully ripe, sweeter, higher in vitamin C and lycopene
- **Yellow/Orange**: Intermediate ripeness, balanced sweetness
- **Purple**: Late-stage ripeness, more bitter

## Nutritional Profile
- Exceptional source of vitamin C (especially red peppers)
- Rich in vitamin A and beta-carotene
- Contains capsaicinoids in spicy varieties
- Good source of folate and potassium
- Contain antioxidants like quercetin and luteolin
- Low in calories (about 30 per 100g for sweet peppers)
- Contains fiber and small amounts of protein

## Health Benefits
- High vitamin C supports immune system and collagen production
- May reduce inflammation, especially in spicy varieties
- Capsaicin may boost metabolism and aid weight management
- Supports eye health with lutein and zeaxanthin
- May reduce risk of heart disease
- May help regulate blood sugar
- Contains compounds that may reduce cancer risk
- May improve joint health (spicy peppers)
- Supports skin health and wound healing

## Growing Information
- **Climate**: Warm season crop; thrive in 70-85°F (21-29°C) temperatures
- **Soil**: Well-drained, fertile soil with good organic matter
- **Water**: Moderate to consistent moisture; avoid waterlogging
- **Sunlight**: Full sun (6-8+ hours daily)
- **Support**: May need support as plants grow heavy with fruit
- **Harvest**: Can harvest green or wait for color development

## Storage
- Store in refrigerator in plastic bags or crisper drawer
- Sweet peppers keep 1-2 weeks; hotter varieties longer
- Can be frozen after slicing or roasting
- Can be roasted, peeled, and frozen in oil
- Store away from ethylene-producing fruits
- Dried peppers can be stored for months in cool, dry place

## Culinary Uses
- Raw in salads, as crudités, and in salsas
- Roasted and peeled for sauces and spreads
- Sautéed with onions as a base for many dishes
- Grilled as a side dish or in kabobs
- Stuffed with rice, meat, or cheese
- Used in fajitas and stir-fries
- Added to soups and stews
- Dried and ground into powder (cayenne, chili powder)
- Pickled for preserves
- Used in hot sauces (spicy varieties)
- Incorporated into curries and Asian dishes
