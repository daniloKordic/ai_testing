# Broccoli

## Overview
Broccoli is a cruciferous vegetable consisting of flowering heads and stalks, belonging to the cabbage family. Known for its dark green color and tree-like appearance, broccoli is a nutritional powerhouse often called a superfood.

## Varieties
- **Calabrese**: The most common commercial variety with large central heads
- **Sprouting**: Produces many smaller heads instead of one large head
- **Romanesco**: Lime-green, fractal-patterned variety with mild flavor
- **Purple Sprouting**: Produces purple-tinged side shoots
- **Waltham 29**: Cold-hardy variety suitable for fall harvest

## Nutritional Profile
- Exceptional source of vitamin C and vitamin K
- Contains sulforaphane, a powerful anti-cancer compound
- Rich in folate and chromium
- Good source of fiber and protein for a vegetable
- Contains lutein and zeaxanthin for eye health
- About 34 calories per 100g
- Contains isothiocyanates with anti-inflammatory properties

## Health Benefits
- Strong anti-cancer properties, especially for colorectal and breast cancer
- Supports bone health with vitamin K and calcium
- Boosts immune system with high vitamin C content
- Promotes heart health and may lower cholesterol
- Supports detoxification processes in the body
- May improve brain function and reduce inflammation
- Supports digestive health with fiber
- May regulate blood sugar levels

## Growing Information
- **Climate**: Prefer cool season; thrive in 65-75°F (18-24°C) temperatures
- **Soil**: Rich, well-drained soil with good organic matter
- **Water**: Consistent moisture; needs 1-2 inches per week
- **Sunlight**: Full sun (6+ hours daily)
- **Spacing**: 18-24 inches between plants
- **Harvest Time**: 70-90 days; harvest when head is firm and tightly budded

## Storage
- Store in refrigerator in a plastic bag or crisper drawer
- Keeps fresh for 5-10 days
- Can be blanched and frozen for up to 1 year
- Store away from ethylene-producing fruits
- Keep stalks attached until ready to use for longer freshness

## Culinary Uses
- Steamed or roasted as a side dish
- Raw in salads and with dips
- Added to stir-fries and Asian dishes
- Blended into soups and purees
- Roasted with garlic and olive oil
- Grilled with lemon and seasonings
- Added to pasta dishes
- Used in grain bowls
- Pickled for preserves
- Incorporated into curries and Asian cuisines
