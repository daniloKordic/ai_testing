# Leafy Greens

## Overview
Leafy greens are nutrient-dense vegetables known for their high content of vitamins, minerals, and phytochemicals. They form the foundation of many healthy diets and come in various types from mild to bitter flavors.

## Common Varieties

### Spinach
- Dark green, tender leaves with mild, slightly sweet flavor
- Contains oxalates (bind calcium)
- Available fresh, frozen, or canned
- Versatile in cooking and raw applications
- Peak season: Spring and fall

### Lettuce
- Mild, crisp vegetable with watery texture
- Varieties: Iceberg, Romaine, Butterhead, Leaf lettuce
- Perfect for salads and sandwiches
- Low in calories, mostly water
- Excellent raw but can be cooked

### Kale
- Sturdy, dark green leaves with prominent veins
- Varieties: Curly, Lacinato, Red Russian
- Peppery, robust flavor
- Extremely nutrient-dense
- Becomes sweeter after frost

### Arugula
- Peppery, spicy flavor
- Tender leaves with distinctive peppery bite
- Becomes more peppery as plant matures
- Best eaten fresh and raw
- Quick-growing crop

### Swiss Chard
- Colorful stems (red, yellow, white, orange)
- Tender green leaves
- Mild, slightly earthy flavor
- Both leaves and stems edible
- Similar nutritional profile to kale

## Nutritional Profile
- Exceptionally high in vitamins K, A, C, and folate
- Rich in iron, calcium, magnesium, and other minerals
- Contain lutein and zeaxanthin for eye health
- Rich in antioxidants and phytonutrients
- High in fiber relative to calorie content
- Contain compounds with anti-inflammatory properties
- Very low in calories (10-40 per 100g)

## Health Benefits
- Support bone health with vitamin K and calcium
- Protect eye health with lutein and zeaxanthin
- Support blood clotting and wound healing
- May reduce risk of heart disease
- May improve bone density
- Contains compounds that may protect against cancer
- Support brain health and cognitive function
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties
- May help regulate blood sugar
- Support immune system function

## Growing Information
- **Climate**: Cool season crops; prefer 60-70°F (15-21°C)
- **Soil**: Rich, well-drained soil with good organic matter
- **Water**: Consistent moisture; avoid letting soil dry out
- **Sunlight**: Prefer partial shade in hot climates; full sun in cool climates
- **Spacing**: Varies by variety; typically 6-12 inches apart
- **Harvest**: Can be harvested continuously; pick outer leaves first

## Storage
- Wash and dry thoroughly before storing
- Store in refrigerator in plastic bags or containers
- Line containers with paper towels to absorb excess moisture
- Keep in crisper drawer; separate from ethylene-producing fruits
- Most last 3-7 days; heartier greens (kale, chard) last longer
- Can be blanched and frozen for extended storage

## Culinary Uses
- Raw in salads and as side greens
- Sautéed with garlic as a side dish
- Added to soups and stews
- Steamed or boiled
- Incorporated into smoothies (especially spinach)
- Used as pizza toppings
- Mixed into pasta dishes
- Added to egg dishes (omelets, frittatas)
- Massaged with oil for salads (especially kale)
- Blended into sauces and dressings
- Used in Asian stir-fries
